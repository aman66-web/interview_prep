### 1b. Short notes on each main competitor

- **CaseCoach.** Founded by a former McKinsey engagement manager (their claim). Says it serves "50+ leading universities in 14 countries" (their claim, [casecoach.com/about-us](https://casecoach.com/about-us)). Prices are shown in pounds, euros and dollars. Yale's careers office gives its students a free CaseCoach login, and lists "AI-powered voice tools" among the drills ([ocs.yale.edu/resources/casecoach](https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/casecoach/)). So a big part of its reach is universities paying for students.
- **PrepLounge.** The biggest peer community. Says it has "over 582,000 like-minded peers" and 500+ coaches (their claim, [preplounge.com/en](https://www.preplounge.com/en)). Its main draw is free peer mock interviews with other candidates. Its public forum is full of candidate questions, mostly answered by coaches who sell sessions. Its own (older) article mentions an AI casebot called "Preppie", but the current pricing page does not mention AI ([preplounge.com/en/articles/case-interview-preparation-chatgpt](https://preplounge.com/en/articles/case-interview-preparation-chatgpt)).
- **RocketBlocks.** Drills only, no AI interviewer mentioned on its consulting page. Strong on maths and chart drills. Has a "For schools" link but no details.
- **MConsultingPrep.** AI case simulator plus online-test simulators for McKinsey Solve, BCG Casey and Bain tests. Big YouTube channel. Has a financial aid application and a universities partnership contact ([mconsultingprep.com](https://mconsultingprep.com/)).
- **Crafting Cases.** Founded by former McKinsey and Bain consultants (their claim). Uses a free 7-day email course to build its list. Sells course licences to universities "with special bulk discounts" and runs live workshops for clubs ([craftingcases.com/universities](https://www.craftingcases.com/universities/)).
- **CaseInterview.com.** Gives away 6 hours of videos in exchange for an email address, then sells the audio programme and coaching. It has a 30-day refund promise ([caseinterview.com/loms](https://www.caseinterview.com/loms)).
- **Management Consulted.** Runs case interview workshops through US university careers centres, for example a virtual 4-hour session listed by UT Austin on 27 Aug 2026 ([graduate.utexas.edu event page](https://graduate.utexas.edu/events/beginning-and-advanced-case-interview-workshop-management-consulted)). Its own site could not be read.

### 3e. What candidates say they want and complain about (summarised in my own words)

From PrepLounge forum threads read today:

1. **No partner, no budget.** A graduate (posted 11 Dec 2025) wanted to prepare without a coach or partner because of money. Their weak spots: building a structure fast under time pressure, asking good clarifying questions, and doing maths and chart reading accurately and quickly. ([thread 24071](https://www.preplounge.com/consulting-forum/how-to-prepare-for-case-interviews-without-a-coach-or-partner-24071))
2. **Coaching is expensive.** A candidate (July 2026) weighed a coaching programme costing over €3,000 against a few single sessions. Almost every reply said: do not buy the big package; buy two or three sessions to find your gaps, then practise cheaply. ([thread 25032](https://www.preplounge.com/consulting-forum/what-do-you-think-about-coaching-programms-what-do-you-think-about-a-couple-of-case-coaching-sessions-25032))
3. **AI tools feel generic.** Coaches (2024 to 2026) said AI practice tools give feedback that is too general, often praise weak structures, are poor at maths and market sizing, and cannot copy the back-and-forth of a real interview. Most saw AI as a supplement, useful when no partner is available. One user (March 2026) complained that a tool's answers were often wrong, its features were paywalled and a refund was refused. ([thread 20398](https://www.preplounge.com/consulting-forum/has-anyone-tried-any-of-the-ai-interview-tools-for-mbb-before-20398), [thread 22042](https://www.preplounge.com/consulting-forum/ai-chatgpt-for-case-practice-22042))
4. **Plain ChatGPT is weak as a case partner.** Several coaches (Feb to May 2025) said general chatbots give shallow structures and long-winded maths, and can make facts up. (thread 22042 above)

Note: these voices are mostly coaches, who have a reason to downplay AI. Treat them as signals, not as proof.

## 6. What this means for Mock Partner

1. **The prices are fine.** $39 a month is a little below the $49 middle of the market. $99 for 90 days equals the cheapest similar offer (CaseTutor) and beats Road to Offer's $120. Do not start by cutting prices.
2. **Timing is urgent.** UK full-time case interviews run from mid-November to early December 2026 (Bain London, official). BCG London internship applications run 1 to 24 January 2027. Every week the launch blockers stay open eats into the busiest selling window inside the 90 days. Christmas is likely quiet (ASSUMPTION).
3. **The AI space is crowded,** with at least 12 AI case tools, several at almost the same price. Mock Partner needs a clear, honest reason to choose it. The candidate complaints suggest one: rivals' AI is called generic, too kind and weak at maths. Mock Partner checks the maths with code and scores five skills. Say this plainly, and only claim what is true and tested.
4. **Text-only is a gap and a possible angle.** Most rivals are voice-first. Text practice can be done quietly (for example in a library), but it is less like a real interview (my reading). Be upfront that it is text.
5. **Content volume is a trust gap.** Rivals show 71 to 200+ cases. Mock Partner has 20 drafts, and the live site shows none until a reviewer signs them off. Getting the free starter case and a first batch reviewed is a sales blocker, not just a quality step.
6. **Copy the channel mix, not the content.** Rivals win through YouTube, free email courses, search articles, university careers services and student societies. A solo founder cannot match 100k-subscriber channels in 90 days. But societies, careers services and one free lead magnet (for example a free drill pack or free case, with opt-in for emails) are within reach.
7. **University and society deals are a real route.** Yale pays for students' CaseCoach access. Crafting Cases and CaseStudyPrep.AI sell bulk licences. Mock Partner already has a "for universities and clubs" enquiry form; pricing and fulfilment would need building.
8. **$10,000 a month inside 90 days is a stretch.** The benchmarks point to roughly 2,000 to 5,000 free sign-ups a month and tens of thousands of visits for a brand-new site. The playbook should set the 90 days as building the engine (launch, first reviewed cases, first 100 to 300 paying customers, ASSUMPTION), with $10,000 as the run-rate target, and report honestly each week against the funnel numbers.
9. **Think about a longer plan later.** Education apps lean towards annual plans (RevenueCat), and most rivals offer a cheap year. A "season" or yearly option could lift sales. This is a suggestion, and Claude can build it if the founder agrees.
10. **Show pounds to UK visitors** (ASSUMPTION that it helps). CaseCoach already does. Today $39 is about £29.
