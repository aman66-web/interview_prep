Subject: Your first-round case interview checklist

Hi,

First-round case interviews for some UK graduate roles start about now. Bain London's careers page, for example, says its first rounds are held in mid-November (https://www.bain.com/careers/recruiting/london-recruiting/). Here's a checklist for the week before yours.

1. One full timed case a day, then 10 minutes reviewing it. Pick one thing to fix next time.
2. Ten minutes of mental maths a day, by hand. McKinsey and Bain both say calculators aren't allowed in their interviews (https://www.mckinsey.com/careers/interviewing and https://www.bain.com/careers/hiring-process/digital-assessment/).
3. Practise the first two minutes: play the question back, then ask about the goal, the timeframe and the client.
4. Practise reading an exhibit out loud: title, units, the one thing that stands out, then the so-what.
5. Practise your close: answer first, three reasons with numbers, the main risk and the next steps, all in under a minute.
6. Read the firm's own careers pages for the interview format, and prepare your 'why consulting' and 'why this firm' answers from them.
7. Check the practical things in your invite: online or in person, the time zone, and your camera and microphone if it's online.

When an interviewer asks 'What could the client do?', group your ideas before you list them. Ten random ideas sound weaker than six ideas in clear groups.

Groups that work for most questions:
- Inside the business vs outside it (partners, buying a company).
- More revenue vs lower costs.
- Quick wins vs longer-term moves.

Say the groups first, then two or three ideas in each, then pick the one you'd try first and say why.
