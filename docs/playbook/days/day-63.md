Blended margins catch a lot of people.

Made-up example: a company sells two products.
Product A: 60% of revenue, 30% margin.
Product B: 40% of revenue, 10% margin.
What's the overall margin?

Not 20% (the simple average). Weight it by revenue: 0.6 × 30% + 0.4 × 10% = 18% + 4% = 22%.

Now the follow-up: next year the split moves to 50/50 and both margins stay the same. The overall margin falls to 20%, even though neither product got worse.

That's a mix effect, and it's a classic answer to 'profit fell but nothing seems to have changed'.
