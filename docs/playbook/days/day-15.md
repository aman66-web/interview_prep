Break-even questions come up often in cases, and the maths is short:

Break-even volume = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit)

An example with invented numbers: a new coffee kiosk has £120,000 of fixed costs a year. A coffee sells for £3.50 and costs £1.10 to make, so each one adds £2.40. It needs 50,000 coffees a year to break even, which is about 137 a day.

The number is the easy part. A strong answer then asks: is 137 a day realistic for that spot, and what would have to be true?
