# Harlow Building Society: what next for its branches?

Case type: profitability · Industry: Retail banking (building society) · Difficulty 2 of 3 · About 30 minutes · Style: interviewer_led

Status: draft (draft until you sign it off). Page on the preview site: /cases/harlow-building-society-branches

## 1. What the candidate is told

You are a graduate in the strategy team of Harlow Building Society, a regional UK building society with 60 branches across the south-west of England. Over the last three years more and more of its members have started doing their banking on its mobile app. Three years ago its branch network made a profit of £13 million; this year it made far less. Harlow's chief executive has asked the strategy team for a view before her board meets: why has branch profit fallen, which branches are the problem, and what should Harlow do with its weakest branches, given that some members still rely on them? How would you approach this?

## 2. Facts the interviewer gives only when asked

**The client.** Harlow Building Society is a regional building society based in the south-west of England, with about 300,000 members and 60 branches. It offers savings accounts and mortgages to individuals; it has no current accounts and no business customers. It launched its mobile app six years ago.

**What a building society is.** A building society is owned by its members, the people who save or borrow with it, rather than by shareholders. Harlow pays no dividends: it keeps its profit to build up its capital, the financial cushion that lets it keep lending safely and absorb any losses. Harlow has no shareholders it could ask for new capital, so the profit it keeps is its main way to build capital. The board aims to make enough profit to keep its capital strong and to pass the rest back to members through better rates. So profit still matters, even though no shareholder receives it.

**How Harlow makes money.** Most of Harlow's income is its net interest income: the interest it earns on the mortgages it lends minus the interest it pays to its savers. Bankers often quote it as a net interest margin, a percentage of the mortgage book. Harlow also earns small fees, for example when it arranges a mortgage. Most of the money Harlow lends as mortgages comes from its members' savings.

**How branch profit is measured.** Harlow's management accounts credit each branch with income for the members it serves: a share of the net interest income on their accounts, plus the fees on accounts it opens. When a member switches to doing most of their banking on the app, the income from their accounts is credited to the digital channel instead, so it does not disappear from Harlow. Over the last three years the income credited to each branch has moved in line with its counter transactions. Branch profit is the income credited minus the branch's own costs.

**Objective.** The board wants the branch network to pay its way, and a plan for its weakest branches within the next year. It is willing to close branches, but it wants to know who would be affected and how they would still get help.

**Harlow's overall profit.** Harlow's overall profit has held up, at about £20 million a year, about the same as three years ago. Most of the income that the branches have lost is now credited to the digital channel, which costs much less to run. The problem is the branch network, not the whole society.

**Members and transactions.** Membership has stayed at about 300,000 over the last three years: members are not leaving. A transaction is any time a member pays in, takes out or moves money, checks a balance or changes their details. Branch figures count only what is done with staff at the counter or desk. Harlow has no current accounts, but many older members use a passbook savings account as their everyday account: their pension is paid into it and they take out cash at the counter, so they visit often.

**Branch types.** Harlow has three types of branch: 10 large branches in town centres, 30 standard high-street branches and 20 small branches in small towns and villages. All 60 open five days a week. Large branches also house Harlow's mortgage and savings advisers, who see members by appointment; the advisers' costs, and the income from the mortgages and savings they arrange, are counted in a separate advice team, not in the branch figures. A small branch has three staff, and Harlow's own security rules need at least two people on site whenever a branch is open.

**Branch costs.** Each branch has three cost lines: staff; premises (rent, business rates, energy and upkeep); and other costs such as cash collection, security and equipment. Branch costs are about the same as three years ago: pay rises were offset by fewer staff hours. Most of the costs are fixed: a branch needs the same building and a minimum number of staff whether it serves 100 members a day or 300.

**Cost of the app and online banking.** Harlow's app and online banking cost about £6 million a year to run, including the IT team and security. That cost has barely changed as use has grown.

**Who uses the small branches.** About 60% of the members who use the small branches regularly are over 70, against about 25% of all Harlow members. About half of these regular users have never logged in to the app or online banking. About a third of counter transactions at small branches involve cash, mostly members taking out cash or paying in cash savings.

**Help that is hard to give by app.** Branch staff say much of their time now goes on help that is hard to give through an app: checking whether a payment request is a scam, sorting out a member's accounts after a death in the family, or explaining savings options face to face.

**Where the small branches are.** 8 of the 20 small branches are within 3 miles of another Harlow branch, with a regular bus between them. The other 12 are more than 10 miles from the nearest Harlow branch, and in 5 of those towns Harlow's branch is the last bank or building society branch left.

**The board's branch pledge.** Harlow's board has made its own promise to members: before closing a branch it consults the members who use it and gives at least 12 weeks' notice, and it will not leave a town where its branch is the last bank or building society branch without some way to get face-to-face help.

**Rules on closing branches.** For this case, treat the board's pledge as everything Harlow must do before closing a branch or changing its hours. The case does not test the real UK rules on branch closures or access to cash, so you do not need to know them.

**What happens when a branch closes.** Harlow closed two branches five years ago, each about 2 miles from another Harlow branch. About 20% of the income from those branches' members was lost, as some members moved their savings elsewhere; the rest stayed with Harlow, using the nearby branch, the telephone, online banking or the app. Closing a small branch now would cost about £100,000 once (redundancy, paying to end the lease early and removing equipment), and all of its yearly costs, premises included, would stop once it closed. Nearby branches have spare capacity, because their own transactions have fallen, so they would not need extra staff.

**Opening fewer days.** Last year Harlow tested opening one small branch three days a week instead of five, for six months. Staff costs fell at a rate of about £50,000 a year, premises and other costs stayed the same, and the branch kept almost all of its transactions, because members came on the days it was open.

**Why members choose Harlow.** In Harlow's own survey, about 1 in 5 savers who joined last year said a local branch was one of the main reasons they chose it. Several other banks and building societies have closed branches in towns where Harlow has branches.

## 3. What a good structure includes

Key buckets:
- Income: counter transactions per branch and the income credited for them, and how that income is measured
- Costs: staff, premises and other costs, and how much of them is fixed
- Channel shift: how members' use of branches, the app, phone and online banking has changed over time
- Profit and break-even by branch type: large, standard and small
- Options for the weakest branches and what each is worth: close, open fewer days, share or replace with other face-to-face help
- Members who rely on branches: who they are, what alternatives they have, the board's pledge and Harlow's reputation

Good signs:
- Starts from branch profit = income credited minus branch costs, and asks how income is credited to a branch
- Notices that total transactions grew while counter transactions fell, so members are switching channel, not leaving
- Sees that branch costs are mostly fixed, so a fall in transactions hits profit almost pound for pound
- Works out profit and break-even per branch type, and spots that standard and large branches are not far above break-even either
- Separates the income credited to a branch from the income Harlow would actually lose if the branch closed
- Asks who uses the weakest branches and what other options they have before recommending closures

Red flags:
- Recommends closing every small or loss-making branch without asking who uses them or how far the next branch is
- Treats all the income credited to a branch as income Harlow would lose if it closed
- Assumes members are leaving Harlow because counter transactions fell
- Recommends keeping every branch as it is because some members need them, without dealing with the losses

## 5. The maths, with working

**Question 1.** What profit did Harlow's branch network make this year, adding up all 60 branches, in £ million?

Inputs: large_branches = 10, large_income = 600, large_staff = 300, large_premises = 150, large_other = 50, standard_branches = 30, standard_income = 300, standard_staff = 180, standard_premises = 70, standard_other = 30, small_branches = 20, small_income = 150, small_staff = 120, small_premises = 40, small_other = 20
Expected answer: 1 (accepted within 5%)
Working: Profit per branch: large £600k - £500k = £100k; standard £300k - £280k = £20k; small £150k - £180k = a loss of £30k. Across the network: 10 x £100k + 30 x £20k + 20 x (-£30k) = £1,000k + £600k - £600k = £1,000k, which is £1 million, down from £13 million three years ago. Income fell from £30 million to £18 million while costs stayed at £17 million.

**Question 2.** By what percentage did branch counter transactions fall over the last three years, across the whole network?

Inputs: counter_then = 6000, counter_now = 3600
Expected answer: 40 (accepted within 2%)
Working: Counter transactions fell from 6.0 million (6,000 thousand) to 3.6 million, a drop of 2.4 million. 2.4 / 6.0 = 40%. Over the same years total transactions rose from 20 million to 30 million, so members moved to the app rather than leaving, and the branches' share of transactions fell from 30% to 12%. Income credited to branches fell by the same 40%.

**Question 3.** Each counter transaction brings a small branch about the same income. How many counter transactions a year would an average small branch need to break even, in thousands?

Inputs: small_staff = 120, small_premises = 40, small_other = 20, small_income = 150, small_transactions = 30
Expected answer: 36 (accepted within 3%)
Working: A small branch is credited with £150k for 30,000 transactions, which is £5 per transaction. Its costs are £120k + £40k + £20k = £180k. £180k / £5 = 36,000 transactions a year, 20% more than the 30,000 it has today, and transactions are still falling. (A standard branch needs £280k / £5 = 56,000 against 60,000 today, and a large branch £500k / £5 = 100,000 against 120,000.)

**Question 4.** Harlow is considering closing the 8 small branches that are within 3 miles of another Harlow branch. If, as in its past closures, it loses about 20% of each closed branch's income and keeps the rest, by how much would closing them raise Harlow's yearly profit, in £ million, before the one-off closure costs?

Inputs: branches_closed = 8, small_staff = 120, small_premises = 40, small_other = 20, income_lost_pct = 20, small_income = 150
Expected answer: 1.2 (accepted within 5%)
Working: Closing a small branch saves all of its £180k of yearly costs (£120k + £40k + £20k). Harlow loses only the income of members who leave: 20% of £150k = £30k. The other £120k stays with Harlow through the nearby branch or the app. So each closure adds £180k - £30k = £150k a year, and 8 closures add 8 x £150k = £1.2 million a year. The one-off cost is 8 x £100k = £0.8 million, paid back in about 8 months.

## 6. Brainstorm

What could Harlow do with its 20 small branches, other than leave them as they are, and how would it look after the members who still rely on them?

Ideas a strong candidate might raise:
- Close the 8 small branches within 3 miles of another Harlow branch, with the 12 weeks' notice and member consultation the pledge promises, and help their members move to the nearby branch or the app
- Open the 12 remote small branches three days a week instead of five: the pilot suggests staff costs fall about £50k and almost all transactions stay, turning a £30k loss into a profit of about £20k
- Test three-day opening in more branches before rolling it out, since the result so far comes from one branch over six months
- Close some or all of the remote branches not covered by the pledge as well: closing a small branch saves more in costs than all the income credited to it, but only with a clear plan for older and cash-using members
- In the 5 towns where Harlow's branch is the last one, keep some face-to-face help: a part-time branch, a counter shared with other providers, a desk in a library or community centre, or a mobile branch that visits on set days
- Run app help sessions in branches, and give members who find the app hard a phone line with a named contact
- Offer video appointments with advisers from small branches, so members can get advice without a large branch nearby
- Find a way for members to keep using cash when a branch closes, such as a partner's local counter, before closing any branch where many members rely on cash
- Move to smaller or cheaper premises when leases come up, and end leases at their break dates
- Act on the standard and large branches now, before they slip into loss: fewer staff hours, smaller premises, opening fewer days, or merging branches that are close to each other
- Turn the large branches into hubs for help that is hard to give by app: advice, scam checks and bereavement
- Judge branches by the total value of the members who use them, not only counter transactions, before deciding which to close
- Use the branches as a reason to join: market them to members of rivals that have closed branches
- Set a yearly review with a clear trigger, for example any branch below its break-even number of transactions

## 7. What a good recommendation includes

Must mention:
- Branch network profit fell from £13 million to about £1 million because counter transactions fell 40% as members moved to the app, while branch costs, which are mostly fixed, stayed at £17 million
- Profit by type: large branches make about £100k each, standard about £20k and small branches lose about £30k each; a small branch needs about 36,000 transactions a year to break even against 30,000 today
- A clear recommendation that treats the small branches differently depending on whether their members have other options, for example closing the 8 near another branch (about £1.2 million a year) and keeping cheaper face-to-face help in the remote towns; a reasoned case for closing the remote branches not covered by the pledge, with a plan for their members, is also acceptable

Good signs:
- Points out that closing a branch only loses the income of members who leave Harlow (about 20% in past closures), not all the income credited to it
- Sees that closing a small branch saves money even if all its income were lost (£180k of costs against £150k of income), so the decision about the remote branches rests on members, the pledge and reputation, and says so
- Warns that standard branches (56,000 transactions needed against 60,000) and large ones (100,000 against 120,000) are not far above break-even, that on the current trend every type of branch would make a loss next year (about £3 million in total), and that the small-branch actions (about £1.8 million a year) only buy time, so the whole network needs a cost plan
- Weighs the older and cash-using members, the 5 towns where Harlow's branch is the last one and the board's pledge, and proposes how to help members move online
- Covers risks and next steps: consultation and 12 weeks' notice, reputation (1 in 5 new savers cite a local branch), staff, and the £0.8 million of one-off closure costs, paid back in under a year
- Compares the cost of a branch transaction (about £4.70) with the app and online banking (about 25p), and notes that the two are not like for like

## 8. Model answer outline

1. Clarify: the board wants the branch network to pay its way and a plan for its weakest branches within a year, without cutting off members who rely on them. Harlow is a mutual, so profit builds the capital it needs to keep lending, and the board can choose to pass value back to members instead of maximising profit. Branch income is an internal credit: when members move to the app, their income moves to the digital channel, and Harlow's overall profit has held up at about £20 million a year.
2. Structure: branch profit = income credited minus branch costs. Income depends on counter transactions; costs are staff, premises and other, mostly fixed. Look at the channel shift, then profit and break-even by branch type, then options for the weakest branches and the members they serve.
3. Diagnosis: total transactions rose from 20 million to 30 million, but counter transactions fell 40% (6.0 million to 3.6 million, from 30% to 12% of the total) while the app grew from 5 million to 20 million. Members are not leaving. Income credited to branches fell 40% (£30 million to £18 million, about £5 per transaction), costs stayed at £17 million, so network profit fell from £13 million to £1 million. A branch transaction costs about £4.70 against about 25p by app or online, though an app transaction is often just a balance check, so the two are not like for like.
4. By type: large branches make about £100k each, standard about £20k and small ones lose about £30k each. A small branch needs 36,000 transactions a year to break even against 30,000; a standard branch needs 56,000 against 60,000; a large branch needs 100,000 against 120,000. If counter transactions keep falling by about 0.8 million a year (about 22% next year), every type of branch, large ones included, would make a loss next year, about £3 million across the network.
5. Weakest branches: close the 8 small branches within 3 miles of another branch. Each saves £180k of costs and loses only about £30k of income (20%), so together they add about £1.2 million a year, for £0.8 million of one-off costs, paid back in about 8 months. Keep the 12 remote branches but open them three days a week, which turns each from a £30k loss into about £20k of profit, an improvement of about £50k each, or about £0.6 million a year across the 12, and keep face-to-face help in the 5 towns where Harlow's branch is the last one, as the pledge requires. On money alone, closing would win: closing a small branch saves more than all the income credited to it, so it pays even if every member left, and closing the 12 remote ones would add about £1.8 million a year at the 20% rate, against £0.6 million for three-day opening. We keep them because the 20% loss comes from closures near another branch, small-branch members are mostly older and many use cash, 5 are the last branch in town, 1 in 5 new savers say a local branch is a main reason they joined, and as a mutual Harlow can accept a lower return to serve its members.
6. Recommendation and next steps: consult members and give 12 weeks' notice; run app help sessions, a phone line and video appointments for older members; and find a way for members to keep using cash. The small-branch plan adds about £1.8 million a year, but the trend would take about £4 million off branch profit next year (from £1 million to a loss of about £3 million), so start a network-wide plan now: fewer staff hours and cheaper premises in standard and large branches, merging branches that are close to each other, and acting on leases as they end. Risks: losing more than 20% of income (members who leave also take savings that pay for Harlow's mortgages), the three-day result coming from one branch over six months, harm to Harlow's reputation (1 in 5 new savers cite a local branch) and the effect on staff. Review every branch each year against its break-even number.
