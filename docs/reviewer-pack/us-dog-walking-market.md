# How big is the US dog-walking market?

Case type: market_sizing · Industry: Consumer services · Difficulty 1 of 3 · About 25 minutes · Style: interviewer_led

Status: draft (draft until you sign it off). Page on the preview site: /cases/us-dog-walking-market

## 1. What the candidate is told

Strollhound is a start-up building an app that connects dog owners with vetted local dog walkers. It keeps 20% of every booking. Before investing, a venture fund wants to know how big the US market for paid dog walking is, and whether Strollhound could reach $50 million a year in revenue within five years. Estimate the market and give the fund your answer.

## 2. Facts the interviewer gives only when asked

**What counts as the market.** Yearly spending by US households on paid dog walks, whether booked through an app, an agency or directly with an independent walker. Leave out boarding, daycare, grooming, overnight pet sitting and walks done for free by friends or family.

**Households.** Assume there are 125 million households in the US.

**Dog ownership.** Assume 40% of households own at least one dog. Count each household once, even if it has several dogs, because one walk usually covers all of a household's dogs.

**Who pays for walks.** Assume 1% of dog-owning households use a walker regularly, for example because they work long days, and 4% use one now and then, for example during busy weeks or after an injury. The rest never pay for walks.

**How often they book.** Regular users book about 4 walks a week for 50 weeks a year, so about 200 walks. Occasional users book about 25 walks a year.

**Price.** Assume an average price of $20 for a 30-minute walk, the same whether it is booked through an app or directly.

**Candidate's own assumptions.** If the candidate suggests their own figure for households, dog ownership, how many households pay for walks, walks a year or the price, say it is a fair guess but ask them to use the case figure instead, so that the numbers can be checked.

**The fund's goal.** The fund wants to see a path to $50 million of yearly revenue within five years. The founders believe Strollhound could handle 10% of all paid dog walks in the US by then.

**Strollhound today.** Strollhound launched about a year ago in three large cities and is still small. Its own figures are not needed: the fund wants to know whether the market is big enough and what share of it Strollhound would need.

**Customers leaving the app.** Some regular customers get to know their walker and then book them directly, which avoids Strollhound's 20% cut. Strollhound does not yet know how often this happens.

**Differences by region.** People in big cities use dog walkers much more than people in rural areas, but a national average is fine for this estimate.

**Market growth.** Ignore growth. Size today's market and apply Strollhound's share to it.

**Competitors.** There are other apps and many independent walkers and small agencies, and many owners book a walker they know directly. Details on competitors are not needed for the estimate.

## 3. What a good structure includes

Key buckets:
- Define the market: yearly spending on paid dog walks in the US
- From households to dog-owning households
- Split dog-owning households by how often they pay for walks, for example regular and occasional users
- Walks per year times the price per walk
- Strollhound's revenue: its share of walks times the 20% it keeps, compared with $50 million

Good signs:
- Uses households, not people or dogs, and counts each dog-owning household once
- Splits regular and occasional users instead of using one average
- States each assumption and rounds numbers to keep the maths simple
- Works backwards from $50 million to the share of walks Strollhound would need
- Answers the fund's question, not just the market size

Red flags:
- Includes boarding, daycare or grooming in the market
- Assumes most dog owners pay for walks
- Treats the market's bookings as Strollhound's revenue, forgetting that it keeps only 20%
- Stops at the market size without working out Strollhound's revenue

## 5. The maths, with working

**Question 1.** How many US households own a dog, in millions?

Inputs: households_m = 125, dog_pct = 40
Expected answer: 50 (accepted within 5%)
Working: 40% of 125 million households is 50 million dog-owning households.

**Question 2.** How many paid dog walks are there in the US each year, in millions?

Inputs: dog_households_m = 50, regular_pct = 1, regular_walks = 200, occasional_pct = 4, occasional_walks = 25
Expected answer: 150 (accepted within 5%)
Working: Regular users: 1% of 50 million = 500,000 households x 200 walks = 100 million walks. Occasional users: 4% of 50 million = 2 million households x 25 walks = 50 million walks. Total 150 million walks a year, two-thirds of them from regular users.

**Question 3.** What is the US market for paid dog walking worth each year, in $ billion?

Inputs: walks_m = 150, price = 20
Expected answer: 3 (accepted within 10%)
Working: 150 million walks x $20 = $3,000 million, which is $3 billion a year.

**Question 4.** If Strollhound handled 10% of all paid walks and kept 20% of each booking, what would its yearly revenue be, in $ million?

Inputs: market_bn = 3, share_pct = 10, take_pct = 20
Expected answer: 60 (accepted within 10%)
Working: 10% of $3 billion is $300 million of bookings. Strollhound keeps 20%, which is $60 million a year, just above the fund's $50 million bar.

**Question 5.** What share of all paid dog walks would Strollhound need to reach $50 million of yearly revenue, in %?

Inputs: target_m = 50, market_bn = 3, take_pct = 20
Expected answer: 8.3333 (accepted within 5%)
Working: $50 million of revenue at a 20% cut needs $250 million of bookings ($50 million / 0.2). $250 million / $3,000 million = about 8.3% of all paid walks, or about 12.5 million walks a year. The founders' 10% clears the bar, but with little room to spare.

## 6. Brainstorm

How could Strollhound win 10% of all paid dog walks in the US?

Ideas a strong candidate might raise:
- Focus on regular weekday users, who make two-thirds of all walks, with weekly subscriptions
- Keep regular customers on the app with lower fees on repeat bookings, and insurance and cover for a sick walker that apply only to app bookings
- Build trust with background checks, insurance, and GPS tracking with photo updates after each walk
- Offer a free or discounted first walk so owners try the app
- Recruit independent walkers by giving them free booking, payment and scheduling tools
- Buy or partner with small local agencies to take on their customers and walkers
- Partner with apartment buildings, vets, pet shops and shelters to reach new dog owners
- Start in dense big cities where demand is highest, then expand
- Offer cheaper group walks to bring in price-sensitive owners
- Add related services such as pet sitting so customers stay on the app
- Sell dog walking to employers as a benefit for their staff

## 7. What a good recommendation includes

Must mention:
- A market of about $3 billion a year: about 150 million paid walks at $20
- At 10% of walks and a 20% cut, Strollhound would earn about $60 million, just above the fund's $50 million bar
- A clear answer for the fund: the market is big enough, but Strollhound needs more than about 8% of all paid walks, so the investment rests on that share

Good signs:
- Points out that regular users make two-thirds of all walks, so they matter most
- Questions whether a young start-up can win 8% to 10% of a market where many walks are booked directly with independent walkers
- Names the risk that regular customers book their walker directly to avoid the 20% cut
- Suggests how to check the estimate and the share, such as surveying dog owners in a few cities or looking at the share Strollhound already has in its launch cities

## 8. Model answer outline

1. Clarify: yearly spending on paid dog walks in the US, leaving out boarding, daycare, grooming and free walks. The fund wants a path to $50 million of revenue within five years for Strollhound, which keeps 20% of each booking.
2. Households: 125 million households, 40% with a dog, gives 50 million dog-owning households.
3. Users: 1% use a walker regularly (500,000 households x 200 walks = 100 million walks) and 4% occasionally (2 million x 25 = 50 million walks). About 150 million walks a year, two-thirds from regular users.
4. Value: 150 million walks x $20 = about $3 billion a year.
5. Strollhound: 10% of walks is $300 million of bookings, and its 20% cut is about $60 million, just above the $50 million bar. Working backwards, $50 million needs $250 million of bookings, about 8.3% of all paid walks (about 12.5 million walks a year).
6. Answer: yes, the market is big enough, but only just: the plan needs Strollhound to win more than 8% of all paid walks within five years, in a market where many owners book walkers directly. Risks: regular customers booking their walker directly to avoid the fee, and rival apps. Next steps: check Strollhound's share and repeat bookings in its three launch cities, and survey dog owners in a few cities to test the 1% and 4% usage figures.
